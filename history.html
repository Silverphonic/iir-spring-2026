The History of Generative Music

AI is a brand-new technology — but, in many ways, the concepts behind it are not. The idea that music can emerge from a system — from rules, chance, machinery, or the environment, rather than solely from a performer's hands — is at least 5,000 years old. Every era of human history has found some new way to set music in motion and let it run. Across the residency, we traced that story — from prehistoric caves to the AI tools — because placing today's technology in its historical context helps us to understand it.

Along the way, we assessed every example through the same four lenses:

EnvironmentalSound generated by nature or by acoustic spaces themselves — wind, water, resonant caves.
AutomatedMachines that make music without a human performing in real time — musical clocks, player pianos, robotic automatons.
Rule-BasedMusic generated by explicit systems and procedures — counterpoint, dice tables, algorithms, even basic scales.
Chance / ProcessMusic shaped by randomness or indeterminate processes — coin tosses, tape loops drifting apart.

Most examples don't sit cleanly in one box. As you explore the list below, ask yourself which, if any, of these categories the historical examples fall into. By the time you reach the end of this timeline, AI stops looking like an alien arrival and starts looking like what it is: the newest chapter of an old story.
