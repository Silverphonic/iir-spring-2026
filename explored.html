What We Explored

Over twelve weeks at Fairview's Digital Innovation Hub, we ran hands-on workshops exploring the benefits, complications, strengths, and weaknesses of AI-assisted music-making. From ambient soundscapes to hip-hop sets, we explored how (and when) we might use this technology to create music in accordance with the guidelines of SOCAN — the Society of Composers, Authors and Music Publishers of Canada, the performing rights organization that registers musical works and collects royalties on behalf of their creators — as a tool to enhance the human creative process, and not replace it. Because SOCAN requires a human author for a work to be registered, their guidelines became our practical yardstick throughout the residency. As part of the exercise, we audited each process (to the best of our abilities) to discuss the legal and ethical status of our creations. Were we the authors? Could we copyright our creations? Were we genuinely expressing our own creativity — or were we sitting back and relying on algorithms to piece together outcomes based on probability instead of emotion?
